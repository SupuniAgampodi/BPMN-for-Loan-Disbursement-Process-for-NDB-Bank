Loan Disbursement Process

1.0 Introduction
This report presents a detailed analysis of the Loan Disbursement Process, examining its current state, identifying existing issues, and proposing a redesigned approach to enhance efficiency while improving the performance indicators. This report aims to provide the organization with a comprehensive review of the process, offering insights into areas that can be optimized to improve the overall loan handling experience for customers and the efficiency of the internal workflow.
The report is structured to include background information about the organization and the loan application process, a detailed As-Is model representing the current process flow, and an analysis of the key issues associated with the existing system. It further offers recommendations for process improvement, including a To-Be model illustrating the proposed enhancements. These improvements aim to reduce bottlenecks, enhance accuracy in document handling, and ensure a smoother transition from loan request initiation to final fund disbursement.
By addressing the identified issues and implementing the suggested recommendations, the organization can achieve a more streamlined and customer-friendly Loan disbursement Process.

2.0 Background about the Organisation

Founded in 1979, National Development Bank PLC (NDB) began as a state-owned development finance institution supporting Sri Lanka’s economic growth and SMEs, later evolving into a fully integrated commercial bank listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange in 2005. Headquartered in Colombo with over 100 branches, NDB offers banking, investment, and insurance services while embracing digital innovation and sustainable banking practices. Guided by its values of Integrity, Customer-Centricity, and Innovation, NDB focuses on financial inclusion, SME empowerment, and green finance to drive national development.

3.0 Background about the process
The process starts when the customer requests a loan after meeting the loan officer. First, the loan officer checks the customer's eligibility for the loan. This is done by checking the CRIB report, Bank Profile and the validity of mortgage property. If the customer is not eligible for the loan, the loan officer informs the manager about this. Then, the manager reviews the reasons for rejections and approves the loan rejection. Then, the customer is informed that the loan is rejected because of the specific reason. 
If the customer is eligible for the loan, then the loan officer gives a list of the required documents and asks the customer to email the documents. When the documents are received, the loan officer checks if all the documents are sent by the customer. If there are any missing documents, the loan officer requests the missing documents until all the documents are ready. Then, the loan officer assesses the documents. 
If the documents are not genuine or satisfactory the manager is informed of the loan rejection. The manager views the reasons for rejection and approves the loan rejection. The customer is notified about the loan rejection. If the documents are genuine and satisfactory, the loan officer records the details of the loan application and the documents in the system.
 The manager is notified to review the loan application. The manager then reviews the loan application. If the loan is approved by the manager, the manager will notify the funds manager and loan officer at the same time.  When the approval notification is received, the fund manager allocates funds for the loan and the loan officer prepares the loan offer letter and sends the offer letter to the customer to sign. The customer signs the offer letter and returns it to the loan officer. The loan officer then sends the signed offer letter to the fund manager, and then the fund manager releases the funds to the customer's bank account and informs the loan officer. The loan office then informs the customer that the loan has been dispersed. 
 
The Process Discovery 
The process discovery method used is interviews. An employee of National Development Bank was interviewed few times using unstructured interview method using WhatsApp calls to gain an understanding of the process. A written description of the process was then requested through an email. 

4.1 Assumptions 
•	All loan rejections require manager's approval, ensuring that decisions are made at a higher level of authority.
•	There is a defined process for notifying customers about loan approvals and rejections, as well as the reasons behind these decisions.
•	The fund manager has the authority to allocate funds based on the manager’s approval of the loan application.
•	Customers will respond promptly to requests for missing documents and will sign the loan offer letter in a timely manner.

4.2 Business Rules
•	Eligibility Verification:
o	A customer must have a valid CRIB report, Bank Profile, and verified mortgage property to be considered eligible for a loan.
•	Rejection Communication:
o	If a loan application is rejected, the loan officer must inform the customer of the specific reasons for the rejection.
o	The manager must approve all loan rejections before communicating to the customer.
•	Document Requirements:
o	The loan officer must provide a complete list of required documents to the customer.
o	If any documents are missing, the loan officer must continue to request the missing documents until all are received.
•	Document Assessment:
o	The loan officer must assess the authenticity and adequacy of all submitted documents before proceeding to record the loan application details.
o	If any document is genuine or satisfactory, the loan must be flagged for rejection.
•	Approval Workflow:
o	The manager must review and approve the loan application after the loan officer records the application details.
o	If approved, the funds manager and the loan officer must be notified simultaneously.
•	Fund Allocation:
o	The fund manager is responsible for allocating funds only after receiving approval notification from the manager.
•	Completion of Loan Process:
o	The loan officer must prepare and send the loan offer letter to the customer after allocated funds.
o	The loan offer must be signed by the customer and returned to the loan officer for final processing.
•	Disbursement Notification:
o	The loan officer must notify the customer once the loan has been successfully dispersed into their bank account.
5.0 Issues with the process

5.1. Manual Tasks
The loan officer should manually check the customer's eligibility by reviewing their CRIB reports, bank profiles, and mortgage details, which can be time-consuming and prone to error. Since the documents are collected through email from the customer, the loan officer has to upload the documents manually to the system. 

5.2. Repetitive Tasks
The loan officer has to repeatedly collect documents from the customer if the documents are missing. This increases lead times, hindering the loan officer's productivity and increasing the organisation's labour cost. Moreover, customer satisfaction will decrease as the loan officer has to contact the customer repeatedly to ask for the missing documents. The quality aspects will be compromised due to these unnecessary repetitive tasks.

 5.3. Communication Issues
From the stages of loan approval notification to the disbursal of funds, different notifications occur by human intervention, which may get delayed if there is a delay in communication between the actors involved. Due to this, the lead time of the process can significantly increase. The internal and external quality might degrade.

5.4. Dependency Issues
The loan officer has to get approval for rejection or approval of the loan application from the loan manager, who reviews and approves the same. This increases the labour cost for the organisation as the work is redundant. Moreover, the internal quality decreases with poor conditions of work and may lead to dissatisfaction due to higher dependency. Apart from that, the unnecessary notification flow between the manager and the loan officer takes considerable time, as the loan officer has to wait for the manager's approval to proceed forward, hindering productivity. Overall dependency issues increase cost and lead time and decrease the overall quality.
 

 
 
6.0 Recommendations for improvement

6.1. Introduction of a Web/Mobile Application for Customers
Introducing a web or mobile application can eliminate Manual and repetitive tasks, which are very time-consuming. Customers can submit loan applications online through a secure web portal or a mobile application and upload all the necessary documents directly into the system. This application offers paperless document management, allowing customers to upload their documents securely. The system will highlight any missing documents and Customers will be able to see which documents need to be uploaded, and if any documents are missing, they will be notified directly on the portal.  The system will automatically check eligibility with the help of pre-defined business rules integrated into the system, significantly increasing the speed of the process and reducing errors. The issue of manual and repetitive tasks can be resolved with this system.

6.1.1. Performance Indicators
Time: The average lead time will be decreased significantly with the new customer application. The new system will significantly reduce service time, that is, the time spent on the process, as the customer has already filled in the application online, and all the repetitive tasks and manual tasks are now eliminated.   The queue time and the time spent waiting on the resources will be decreased as the customers have to submit all the necessary documents through the web application.  The new system eliminates a loan officer's repetitive and manual tasks, such as collecting missing documents from customers, performing eligibility checks and conversing with the customer. With the web application, loan officers will not need to re-enter customer details into the system, as the customer can submit a complete application with all the necessary documents. 
Cost: As the lead time decreases significantly, the labor cost will decline. However, there is a tradeoff between human and system costs. Introducing the new system might be more costly than saving the labor cost. But in the long run, it might outweigh the labor cost. Also, we have to consider other performance indicators that will positively affect with the introduction of the new application.
Quality: Eliminating repetitive and manual tasks will decrease the lead time there by increasing both internal and external quality. Externally, customer satisfaction will increase as they will no longer need to visit the bank to apply for a loan. The number of visits will be greatly reduced thanks to the introduction of the web portal. As an added feature, the web portal can integrate a chatbot to answer general customer inquiries, increasing customer service. Internally, the quality of work conditions will improve significantly. Loan officers will no longer need to wait for documents, spend time conversing with customers, or record details in the system. A complete loan application will be readily available, including all documents, allowing loan officers to focus on processing applications.

6.2. Integration of Notification Management System
Integrating an automated notification management system into all the bank applications, including the new customer web/mobile application, will prompt notifications and status updates to all concerned parties at every stage of the process, ensuring timely responses and enhancing the overall customer experience. All notifications sent to customers can be managed through the web portal, where customers can view the current status of their applications. Automated notifications will be sent to customers when approvals are needed, significantly reducing the workload of the loan officer. As the mobile application can be accessed online and push-up notifications can be enabled, the customer can respond to the approvals promptly. The notification management system will help the employees ask for approvals or notify different departments like the fund manager. The communication issues can be resolved by integrating a proper notification management system.

 6.2.1. Performance Indicators
Time: The average lead time will decrease significantly as customers can get notifications through the application and respond to them promptly. The new system eliminates repetitive and manual tasks, such as notifying the customers about the current loan stage or getting documents signed. 
Cost: As the lead time decreases significantly, the loan officer has more time to work on new loan applications. The productivity will be increased, and the labour cost will decline. 
Quality: Eliminating repetitive and manual tasks will decrease the lead time there by increasing both internal and external quality. Externally, customer satisfaction will increase as they can monitor the loan application status. There will be smooth communication throughout the process for both the customer and loan officer, which in turn increases the overall quality.



 
6.3. Empowering Decision-Making Authority
In the current organisational hierarchy, loan officers must obtain approval for all rejections and approvals from their managers, which is common in many non-western organisations. To improve this situation, the organisation can adopt resource best practices that empower loan officers with more responsibility and decision-making authority by reducing middle management involvement. Another resource best practice that the company can adopt is minimising the numerical involvement of the people involved in the processing. With the empowerment of the employees, the number of people is also minimised, time and labour costs are decreased, and quality is increased. 

6.2.1. Performance Indicators
Time: The dependency between loan officers and managers often causes delays. The same work is repeated twice as the manager also reviews the application. By allowing loan officers the authority to make decisions, thereby reducing the number of people working on the process, the average lead time will be significantly reduced. 
Cost: As the lead time decreases, the labour cost will also decline. Empowering best practices will have the most significant impact on flexibility.  However, the trade-off would be that the quality of decisions may be reduced, and errors may not be found.
Quality: These best practices will create a smooth flow of activities for the loan officer, resulting in better work conditions and increasing internal quality. Loan officers will feel more empowered, in control, and have a greater sense of belonging in the organisation rather than relying on their managers. Consequently, managers and loan officers can handle more tasks now as they have more time, improving overall flexibility within the organisation. 


Sustainability Performance Indicator
The sustainability aspect needs to be considered when introducing new systems to automate tasks. However, the tradeoff will be that the cost will go higher. In the long run, there is a possibility that the costs incurred outweigh the positive impacts of sustainability. And with all the performance indicators, there is a tradeoff. Hence, the decision lies with the organisation, which performance indicators they want to prioritise. This should be thoroughly analysed before moving forward with the process redesign. This report will provide a reasonable basis for the executives to understand the aspects involved in the redesign.
